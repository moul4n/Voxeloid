# Visual unlock brief

Visual progression should change the world more than it changes the amount of interface chrome. Each important unlock needs a lasting visual consequence, but only ignition should dominate the whole screen.

## Opening frame

Show the player Core or singularity against a near-black field. Keep the material orb, one target and its short guidance bubble. Do not show decorative stars, a complete talent tree, heat controls or later stellar structures.

The Core should read as small and deliberate beside the first grains. Its physical boundary can remain unchanged while art explores a smaller bright centre, faint containment edge and folded internal filaments. Do not imply that deposits float over empty space.

## Unlock language

New interface sections should grow from the thing they describe. A mass bar can trace out from the first settled material. The Core panel can open from a ring around the Core. The talent area can appear as a small connected constellation rather than a full menu arriving at once.

Guidance uses a dismissible bubble with a line to a semantic target. `Next` moves the same bubble to the new target. Dismissal must persist and must not block the action.

## Impact levels

- Quiet: a new value or label fades and slides into place over 0.25 to 0.5 seconds.
- Medium: the body or Core reacts locally for 0.8 to 1.5 seconds and leaves a new persistent detail.
- High: a talent or permanent layer changes local silhouette, motion or colour over 1.5 to 3 seconds.
- Major: ignition may reframe the camera, change the background, alter the music and hold the new star state. Reserve this level for rare transformations.

## First Matter art placeholders

- First release: a short inward trail makes the grain easy to follow.
- First settled mass: a thin warm trace remains on the body and introduces the mass bar.
- First Core feed: one filament folds inward and stays visible.
- First talent: highlight the compact Core point box first. Clicking it opens and explains the first playable row. Later layers may be visible only as dim, unexplained placeholder nodes.
- First ambient capture: reveal only the nearby capture path. Decorative deep-space particles remain absent.
- Compaction ready: a restrained ring pulse marks the loose shell and points to the action.
- First permanent layer: the layer gains a clear material edge, the camera range widens and the scene gains modest depth.

## Later placeholders

Heat should move from a numeric bar into body colour, convection and surface motion. Rotation needs visible directional features before its indicator appears. The faint star field arrives after three Core points are spent. Its reveal frames the Core as becoming heavy enough to pull distant matter closer, foreshadowing asteroids and other bodies that may be dangerous. Distant system scale still waits for later progression. Ignition should replace the opening darkness with a warmer activity envelope, not merely add a white flash.

The stellar core uses continuously moving molten bands rather than a static rock texture. Bright fissures counter-flow through darker material. Two thin procedural flame contours travel around the core boundary, form uneven heat peaks and fade into the next stellar band. A small stable set of shader embers lifts away and disappears there. These are visual heat cues only: they allocate no particles, change no mass and do not imply that stellar rotation has unlocked.
